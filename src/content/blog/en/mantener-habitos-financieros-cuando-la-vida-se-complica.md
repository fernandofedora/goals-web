If this month you already spent more than planned and feel like the habits you were building went out the window, you're not alone. It has happened to me several times too and the key is not to throw everything away because of one stumble.

## Why habits break when life gets tough

The reality is that a medical emergency, a car breaking down or even a stressful work week can throw off everything you planned. It's not lack of discipline, it's that life doesn't follow the same pace as your spreadsheet.

What has worked for me is accepting that the habit doesn't have to be perfect every day. Sometimes it's enough to keep the intention even if the amount is smaller.

### Start again without guilt

The trick is not to punish yourself for what already happened. If you broke the habit of saving 500 córdobas a week, start again with 100. The goal is to make the action part of your routine again, not to make it big from day one.

## Small adjustments that actually stick

When everything is messy, reduce the friction. Instead of reviewing all expenses every night, try just noting what you spent on food. Something worth trying is using a voice note on your phone at the end of the day.

That said, if you have kids or live with more people, tell them you're trying to regain control. Sometimes just saying it out loud makes you feel less pressure.

## Look ahead without forgetting the present

Watch out for this: it's not about ignoring what's happening now. If you have debts or overdue bills, first cover the basics and then return to the saving habit even if it's minimal.

## Before you close this tab

If you've been saying for weeks that you're going to restart your habits and still haven't done it, it's not that you lack willpower. It's that the plan you had no longer fits with how your life is right now. That has a solution.

*The best habit isn't the strictest one, it's the one you can pick up again next week even if everything feels upside down today.*
