If you've ever felt that "money never stays in my hands" or that "only people born with luck manage to have something," you're not alone. Those phrases we repeat without thinking come from somewhere and end up guiding how we spend, save, or avoid talking about money.

## Where these ideas come from

Most money beliefs we learned as kids. Maybe your mom said "whoever saves, saves for the thief" or your uncle repeated that "banks always win." Those sentences get into your head and then, when payday arrives, you already know how you're going to act.

Watch this: it's not about blaming the family. It's about noticing the pattern. Once you see it clearly, you can decide whether you want to keep carrying that same idea or not.

## Three common beliefs I see all the time

The first is "I'll always be short." This makes you spend everything quickly because "it won't be enough anyway." The second is "saving is for those who earn a lot." And the third, very Nicaraguan, is "you shouldn't show off what you have." This last one even stops you from celebrating when you achieve something.

What has worked for me is writing them down. Take a sheet and write three phrases you repeat about money. Then ask yourself: is this true or is it what I heard all my life?

## How to start changing them without drama

You don't need an overnight transformation. Start with something small. If you believe "money slips away from me," try saving a thousand córdobas the first week you get paid. No more. Just to prove to your head that you can hold onto something.

That said, if you already have some savings and still feel it's not enough, maybe the belief is another: "I'll never have what I need." In that case, it helps to look back and see how far you've come even if it's little.

## For those starting from zero and those who already have a base

If you're starting, the first step is just naming the belief. Don't try to change everything at once. If you already have basic control of your money, you can go further: ask yourself what belief prevents you from taking the next step, whether asking for a raise or starting to invest in something simple.

## Before closing this tab

If you've been repeating the same phrases about money for years and feeling that nothing changes, it's not laziness. It's that no one taught you to question what was put in your head since you were a kid. That has a solution.

*The first step isn't to save more — it's to realize what belief you're carrying without noticing.*
